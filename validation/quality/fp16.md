# fp16 validation

Measured 2026-11-11 on an M4, uncontended, against the released checkpoint
(`model.safetensors`, sha256 `5b549dd6…c68fb0`). Every arm ran strictly
sequentially: two MLX processes on one GPU inflate whatever either of them
reports.

## What was wrong first

`RFD4_DTYPE=fp16` cast the checkpoint and changed nothing else. Three float32
`MLXArray` scalars in the pair-biased attention — the `-1e4` mask fill, the
zero-row fill, and the `dimHead^-0.5` scale — promoted layer 0's output back to
float32, and the recycle feedback carried float32 through every pass after it.
MLX promotes a mixed matmul to its wider operand, so the flag bought a smaller
resident model and no arithmetic in half at all.

Fixed in `Layers/PairBiasAttention.swift` (scalars take the activation dtype) and
`Trunk.swift` (activations cast to the weight dtype at the boundary, outputs cast
back to fp32 so the 60-step flow integration stays in full precision). Both inert
when the weights are fp32.

Same contig (`60-80, /0, A190-339`), same binary, one precision each, paired on

### The designs themselves

`fp16-vs-fp32-binder-structures.txt`. **20 of 24 seeds produce the same design**
— median 0.017 Å fitted CA RMSD, worst 0.101 Å, 95–100% sequence identity.

The other four land on a neighbouring sample (0.57, 0.97, 0.98, 2.79 Å). That is
what a chaotic sampler does when a 1e-3 perturbation crosses a basin boundary,
and it is not a defect: the oracle scores them as equivalent, and the one seed
that fails designability fails in both precisions.

This comparison is the point. Folding both arms and comparing mean designability
would show two equivalent distributions and say nothing about whether fp16
reproduces fp32 *on a given seed* — a generator that produced entirely different
but equally good designs would pass that test. 20/24 sample-identical is the
claim worth making and it needs the structural comparison to make it.

### Designability (Boltz-2 refold of each design's own sequence)

`fp16-binder-designability.csv` / `fp32-binder-designability.csv`.

matching seeds the mean |scRMSD difference| is 0.103 Å.

## Partial diffusion

The one place the fixtures complain: the partial-diffusion trajectory in fp16
exceeds the 5e-2 gate on `local_latents` at max rel 2.6e-01. That is 10 elements
of 7200 (0.14%), with mean |Δ| 1.4e-03 and the backbone inside 0.13 Å.

It does not survive the decoder. `fp16-vs-fp32-partial-structures.txt`: partial
diffusion re-run in both precisions, fractions 0.3/0.5/0.7/0.9 × 3 seeds,

- worst CA RMSD **0.064 Å**, median 0.029 Å
- sequence identity 98–100%
- **no growth with fraction** — 0.9 re-runs three times as much of the trajectory
  as 0.3 and lands no further away, which is what rules out a compounding
  precision floor

`local_latents` is an intermediate the decoder expands into coordinates, so a
max-element bound on it is dominated by outliers the decoder absorbs. The backbone
channel keeps the real gate and passes at 5.2e-03.

## Conclusion

fp16 is not bit-equivalent to fp32 and never will be. It is distributionally
equivalent, usually sample-identical, 1.3x faster, and half the peak memory — and
where exhausting GPU memory aborts the host process rather than throwing, halving
the peak is a safety property rather than an optimization.

fp16 is the default. `RFD4_DTYPE=fp32` opts out, for when a result has to be
reproducible against the fixtures rather than fast.

bf16 was rejected earlier on accuracy at identical speed (8x worse on the pair
track); see the `Precision` doc comment in `Weights.swift`.
